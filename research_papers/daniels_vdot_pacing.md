# Jack Daniels' VDOT Pacing Methodology

## 1. Purpose of VDOT
VDOT is a practical measure of running economy combined with VO2max derived from a recent race performance (e.g., 5K, 10K, Half Marathon). Every training intensity targets a specific physiological adaptation—never run faster than the prescribed zone on recovery or easy days.

## 2. Training Zones & Target Effort
- **Easy (E) Pace (~59%–74% VO2max | 65%–79% HRmax | RPE 2–4/10):** Builds capillary density, mitochondrial volume, and tendon resilience. Conversational effort; slow down further in heat or when fatigued.
- **Marathon (M) Pace (~75%–84% VO2max | 80%–89% HRmax | RPE 5–6/10):** Race-specific endurance and glycogen sparing, typically embedded in segments of long runs.
- **Threshold (T) Pace (~83%–88% VO2max | 88%–92% HRmax | RPE 6–7/10):** "Comfortably hard" ~60-minute race pace; improves lactate clearance. Keep continuous T-runs to 20–30 minutes or cruise intervals (e.g., 3–4 x 2 km with 1 min rest), capped at **<= 10% of weekly volume**.
- **Interval (I) Pace (~95%–100% VO2max | 97%–100% HRmax | RPE 8–9/10):** 3–5 minute repeats (800m–1200m) with equal/slightly shorter jog recovery. Cap total I-volume per session at **8% of weekly mileage or 10 km**, whichever is less.
- **Repetition (R) Pace (>100% VO2max | RPE 9/10):** Short 200m–400m neuromuscular economy strides with full recovery (2–3x work duration).
