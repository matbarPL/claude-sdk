# Achilles Tendinopathy & Workload Load Management (Silbernagel, Alfredson & Gabbett)

## 1. Silbernagel & Alfredson Pain-Monitoring Model for Achilles Tendinopathy
Tendon collagen adapts to controlled mechanical tension, while compressive/high-rate elastic loads (fast track intervals, steep hills, sudden tempo spikes) aggravate reactive tendons.
- **Green Zone (Pain 0–3/10):** Mild stiffness/ache during warm-up that settles during easy flat running and returns to baseline within 24 hours is acceptable to continue easy aerobic training.
- **Yellow/Red Zone (Pain > 3–4/10, sharp twinge, or increased next-morning stiffness):**
  - Immediately **remove speed work (I/R intervals), steep hills, and hard tempo runs** for 7–14 days.
  - Replace missed intensity with **isometric calf holds** (e.g., 5 x 45s mid-range seated/standing) for analgesia, progressing to **heavy slow resistance (HSR) calf raises** (straight-knee + bent-knee soleus work, 3x/week).
  - Keep running strictly **easy, flat, and short** only if pain stays <= 3/10 and next-morning stiffness does not worsen; otherwise substitute low-impact cross-training (cycling/pool running) and advise sports-physio evaluation if pain persists.

## 2. Gabbett Acute:Chronic Workload Ratio (ACWR)
- **Formula:** `ACWR = Acute Load (last 7 days km or sRPE) / Chronic Load (rolling 28-day average weekly load)`.
- **0.8 – 1.3 (Sweet Spot):** Optimal progression zone with minimal soft-tissue injury spike risk.
- **1.3 – 1.5 (Caution Zone):** Elevated tendon/bone stress; avoid adding both volume and intensity simultaneously.
- **> 1.5 (Danger Zone):** High spike risk for Achilles, plantar fascia, and tibial bone stress—trigger an immediate cutback day or volume reduction.
- **< 0.8 (Undertraining / Detraining):** Tissue capacity drops if sustained over multiple weeks.
