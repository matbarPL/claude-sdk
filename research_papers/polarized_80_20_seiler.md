# Polarized Endurance Training (80/20 Model) — Stephen Seiler Synthesis

## 1. Three-Zone Intensity Distribution
Stephen Seiler's research on elite and recreational endurance runners demonstrates that optimal performance and autonomic recovery occur under a **polarized intensity distribution (~80/20)**:
- **Zone 1 (Low Intensity, ~80% of total sessions/volume):** Below the first lactate/ventilatory threshold (**LT1 / VT1**). Typically conversational pace, <77% HRmax, RPE 2–4/10.
- **Zone 2 (Moderate / Threshold, ~5%–10% or used selectively in tempo blocks):** Between **LT1 and LT2 (MLSS / ~85%–90% HRmax)**. Excessive unscheduled time in Zone 2 ("gray zone" running) accumulates sympathetic fatigue without maximizing VO2max or capillary adaptations.
- **Zone 3 (High Intensity, ~10%–15%):** Above **LT2 / critical velocity** (90%–100% HRmax, RPE 8–9/10), executed in structured intervals with full recovery.

## 2. Aerobic Decoupling & Cardiac Drift
- **Definition:** Rise in heart rate relative to pace (or drop in pace at constant HR) between the first half and second half of a steady aerobic run.
- **Threshold:** Aerobic decoupling **<= 5%** indicates strong aerobic durability at that pace. Decoupling **> 5%–8%** signals insufficient aerobic base, glycogen depletion, heat stress, or accumulating fatigue—keep subsequent easy runs strictly in Zone 1 until decoupling normalizes.

## 3. Safe Weekly Volume Progression
- **Weekly ramp cap:** Increase total weekly volume by **<= 10% per week** (often 5%–8% for runners with prior soft-tissue history).
- **Periodized cutback week:** Schedule a recovery/deload week (**-20% to -25% volume**) every **4th week** (3:1 loading cycle) to consolidate mitochondrial and connective-tissue remodeling.
